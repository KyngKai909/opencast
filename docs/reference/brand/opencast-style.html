Ch. 02Mark

A screen, and the light that says it's on.

The mark is a picture tube with its tally lit. The wordmark is Archivo at its widest, set in lowercase, with no separately drawn lettering.

Provisional. Opencast is a working name, so the mark is drawn to survive a rename.

Outline or solid by size

96px / 15mm

Outline from 96px on screen or 15mm in print. Below that the stroke closes up, so use the solid mark, which has the same silhouette with the light cut out.

The light

Tally red

In colour, the dot is the only red in the mark. In one colour, it takes the ink. It never pulses or blinks.

Clear space

¼ of the mark's height

Keep that margin on every side. On a station's picture, the Opencast mark never appears. The station's own bug does.

Wordmark

Archivo 800, width 125

Lowercase, tracking −3.5%, set at the mark's full height. The gap between them is 0.42 of the type size.

Don't

  • Recolour the screen, or put the red anywhere but the light
  • Add waves, antennas or signal arcs
  • Animate the light to blink or pulse
  • Set the wordmark in capitals or retype it

Ch. 04Type

Wide for the ident, plain for the rest.

Three faces, each with one job. A wide grotesque for names and headlines, the way station idents were set. A public-service sans for everything people read. A mono only for what a clock or a dial produces. All three are free and open-licensed.

Ch. 07The tally light

A tally doesn't blink. It's on or it's off.

In a studio, the red light on top of a camera means that camera is the one going out. Opencast uses it the same way: it lights for the one picture that is actually on your screen, and for a broadcaster's station while it's signed on.

  • Light it only when something is really going out, or really on your screen.
  • One per view. Many stations are on air at once; only one is on yours.
  • It is always the words "On air". The colour is never the only signal.
  • It never pulses. It switches on once, with a single flicker, and holds. With reduced motion, it simply appears lit.

Anatomy

Sign

26px tall, 3px corners

44px tall in master control. Padding of 10px, or 18px at the large size.

Word

Archivo 800, 12px

Width 112, capitals, +9% tracking. Capitals are kept for the sign, call signs and log codes.

Glow

14px, 55%

A 2px spread of the tally colour, 35% on the light ground. No glow when unlit.

Switch-on

1.6s, once

Unlit, a brief light, a flicker off, then lit and held. It never repeats.

Video stations take a channel from 2 to 69. A station that carries another around the clock puts it on a subchannel: 12.2, 12.3.

The radio band

Audio stations take a frequency, in odd tenths, like FM. You can tell the band from the number alone.

Call signs

Three to five capital letters, unique across Opencast. No K or W prefix, so no one mistakes one for a licensed station.

Carriage terms

Barter

The default. No fee. Ad time inside the program is split between the station airing it and the station that made it.

Cash

The airing station pays the producer's syndication rate and keeps all the ad time.

Cash plus barter

A lower rate, plus a share of the ad time.

Ch. 09Voice

Talk like the booth, not the lobby.

Calm, exact and a little dry, like an announcer between programs. Plain words, short sentences, the time always given.

Use the station's words

Sign on at 6:00 am. Your log runs until 11:40 pm.
Go live now! Your queue has 17h of content.

Give the time

Next on 7.1 at 9:30: Planning Commission.
Coming up soon on this channel.

Stand by. The signal from BEAT dropped. Reconnecting.
Oops! Something went wrong.

Shared by REEL.

Barter terms: no fee, and the ad time is split.
Carry on barter.

Times

12-hour clock with a lowercase am or pm on its first use in a row: 8:00 pm, 8:30, 9:00. Durations as :30, 28:30, 1:02:15.

Names

Opencast is the platform. A station is never "an Opencast". Stations are named by call sign, then channel: BEAT 12.1.

Paragraphs

Three or four lines on a phone. No exclamation marks and no emoji in the interface.

Ch. 10When there's no picture

Stand by is a message, not an apology.

Television already solved empty screens: say what's happening and when it will change. Every state without a picture follows that, including the moment between channels. The full motion spec is in the changing channel reference.

Ch. 11On screen and off

Keep it inside the safe area.

A station's picture can end up on a phone, a laptop or a television through a translator. Graphics stay inside the broadcast safe areas so nothing is cropped anywhere.
