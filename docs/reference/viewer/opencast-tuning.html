opencast Reference: changing channel
Motion 01Viewer app, TV mode and the radio band

Changing channel should feel like turning a dial.

The one piece of motion the viewer sees most. When someone changes channel, the picture breaks into soft static and the new channel's number appears in the corner, the way an old set's display did. The static lasts at least a moment, so every change feels deliberate, and it covers the time the next channel takes to load. On the radio band, the needle sweeps and a little hiss plays between stations.

Try it below: change channel, turn on a slow connection, or switch to reduced motion.

A click, not a spinner

Loading is shown as tuning. There's never a spinner or a progress bar over the picture.

Static means changing, bars mean trouble

Static is only for changing channel. The colour bars stay reserved for a lost signal, so viewers always know which is which.

Soft, never a strobe

Low-contrast grain with a steady brightness. It never flashes, and it's replaced by a crossfade for anyone who asks for less motion.

Quiet unless it's radio

The hiss plays on the radio band. On video it's off unless the viewer turns "Tuning sound" on.

Tuning in
ON AIR

Corner number first

The channel number and call sign appear the instant the button is pressed, before anything loads, so the viewer always knows where they're going.

The banner comes after

When the first frame arrives, the static clears and the usual banner slides in with what's on and what's next.

Holding down the button

Pressing repeatedly skips ahead: the number updates on each press, the static keeps running, and only the channel you land on loads.

02Frame by frame

Five moments of one channel change.

What happens between pressing channel up on BEAT 12.1 and watching SAZN 18.1, including the slow case and the reduced-motion version.

Never less than 300 ms

Neighbouring channels are preloaded, so up and down are often instant. The static still runs 300 ms so a change never looks like a glitch.

Slow loads say what's coming

After 800 ms the program's name appears over the static. After 8 seconds with no picture, it becomes the Stand by screen with the colour bars, because by then it really is a signal problem.

The same on every screen

Phones, the web player, TV mode, Chromecast and AirPlay all draw the same effect, sized to the screen. On a TV, the corner number is set large enough to read across a room.

03The radio band

The needle sweeps, and there's a little hiss.

On the radio band the frequency is the picture, so tuning is the needle moving along the scale to the next station, with a short hiss between stations. The hiss is on by default here, because on radio it's part of the experience; it can be turned off.

88.3NITE, Night Desk

A sweep, not a jump

The needle travels the real distance between frequencies in 400 ms, easing in and out, passing over the empty band between them.

Hiss, then the station

A short, quiet burst of noise plays while the needle moves, then the station fades up. Sound starts only after the listener has tapped something, as browsers require.
