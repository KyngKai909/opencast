Where the dial's links go.

Four pages the home file already points at: a station's own page, a program's page, search, and the radio band. Each answers one question: who is this station, where can I watch this, how do I find something, and what's on the radio.

A schedule, not a library

Station and program pages show when things air. Nothing plays on demand; everything leads to tuning in or a reminder.

Carriage is visible

Every page names who made a program and who airs it, so the network between stations is something viewers can follow.

Numbers tune

Typing a channel or frequency in search tunes to it, the way a keypad did.

Still no scores

Member counts and carriage counts are the only numbers. No views, no ratings, no rankings.

The header is the station's

Its colour, its channel and call sign in white, and its own one-line description. Buttons on the colour use white and outline so they pass contrast on any station colour.

The week, not an archive

Day tabs over the station's listings. Past programs today are faded, the one on air has the tally edge, and each future program opens its listing with Remind me.

Its place in the network

What it carries from other stations, and what it makes that others carry. The carriage count is the only measure of a station anywhere a viewer looks.

Made possible by

Underwriters and members, exactly as they're read on air. Businesses running spots aren't listed; underwriting is a public credit, spots aren't.

The maker first

The page leads with REEL, the station that made it, even when you arrived from BEAT. Carrying a program never hides its source.

The primary action is what's on

If it's airing anywhere in your market right now, the button tunes there. If not, it becomes Remind me for the next airing.

Episodes lead to airings

Each episode shows its next airing and a reminder. There's no play button: an episode is watched when a station airs it.

On demand

This is the page on-demand viewing would attach to, if it's ever added. The earlier question about watching from the start lives here too.

03Search

Type a name, a program or a channel.

Search takes three kinds of query. A channel or frequency tunes straight away. A call sign or station name finds the station. Anything else finds programs, ordered by when they air next, so what's on now comes first.

Ordered by time, not relevance

A program on now beats one next month. Within the same time, programs in your market come before carried copies elsewhere.

Search opens over everything

The slash key or the search bar opens a full-width field. Esc returns to exactly where you were, and the player keeps going.

Listed programs appear too

City streams Opencast lists but doesn't restream are searchable, marked "listed from the city's stream".

04Radio band

A tuner, then the stations on it.

The Radio nav opens the radio band as a dial: a scale from 88 to 108 with each station marked at its frequency, and a needle where you're tuned. Below it, the same stations as rows with what's on now and next.

The scale is the navigation

Clicking a station on the scale tunes it; the needle slides there. Gaps on the scale show open frequencies, which is also how a would-be station sees there's room.

The tally follows you

The station you're tuned to gets the tally edge in the list and the needle on the scale, and the player bar shows its frequency.

Keeps going in the background

Radio is built to be heard with the tab hidden or the phone locked, with channel up and down on the lock screen.

Radio band.

Channel shortcut first

Typing digits puts a "Tune to" row above the results. One tap tunes; it's the phone version of the TV keypad.

Radio stays on the Dial tab

The radio band opens from the TV and Radio switch on the dial, so it keeps the Dial tab selected. There's no fifth tab.

A swipeable scale

The band scale scrolls sideways with the needle kept in view, and tapping a station mark tunes it.
