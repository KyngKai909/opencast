opencast Reference: home and the dial
Ref. 01Viewer app, web and phone

Home is a dial, not a feed.

This is the reference for the viewer side of Opencast: home, tuning in, the guide and every modal that opens from them, on the web and in the phone app. Built on the style guide's tokens and components. It's 8:42 pm on a Saturday in the Inland Empire, and every station, program and business is an illustration.

The screens are live. Scroll inside them, and switch the ground at the top to see both themes.

Ordered by the dial

Your market's stations are listed by channel number, the same every time. Nothing is ranked by an algorithm, so people learn where things are.

Live and local first

The top slot goes to something live in your market. Carried and national programming comes after it.

You join where it is

Tuning in lands mid-program, like a real station. There's no scrub bar and no restart.

No scores

No view counts, star ratings or trending lists anywhere a viewer sees. A station is judged by its schedule.

The hero is a muted preview

It's labelled "Preview, muted" and has no tally, because it isn't on your screen until you tune in. The one lit tally on home is in the player bar, which is.

What gets the hero

Live programming in your market first, then a live program carried into your market, then whatever your first preset is airing. Never a pick by popularity.

Presets are keys 1 to 6

Like a car radio. On the web, pressing a number tunes that preset from anywhere in the app. More than six are kept in Presets, without keys.

The dial is a list, not cards

One row per station in channel order: what's on, when it ends and what's next. A click tunes in. The ident opens the station preview.

Listed, not carried

RDLS 9.1 is a city's own public-access stream. Opencast lists it and plays the city's player, but doesn't restream it. The dashed "Listed" tag says so.

Home. Scroll inside the phone.

Four tabs

Dial, Guide, Search, You. Radio isn't a tab; it's a band on the dial, switched with the TV and Radio control, because it's the same kind of thing on a different number.

Presets become a strip

Six buttons in a horizontal row, numbered, with the one playing filled in ink. Tap to tune, press and hold to reassign.

The mini player keeps the tally

It stays lit while something plays, including with the screen locked. Swiping the mini player up opens the full player.

Rows drop the next program

At phone width a row is channel, call sign, what's on and until when. Next moves into the station preview.

No scrub bar

A broadcast has no seeking. Pause holds your place for up to 30 minutes, then the player offers "Back to live" rather than piling up a delay.

Changing channel

Up and down move along the dial in channel order, 9.1 to 12.1 to 18.1, with arrow keys on the web. The number between the buttons is always the station you're on.

Carriage is a link

"Carried from REEL 24.1" opens the carried-from modal. The picture carries BEAT's bug, because BEAT is the station airing it.

The member count is the exception

It's a count of supporters, not viewers, and it only appears next to Pledge.

Watch from the start

Whether a program that's already started can be watched from the beginning on demand. Leaving it out keeps the broadcast feel; adding it helps late joiners of long council meetings.

Swipe, with a number

Swiping up or down on the picture changes channel. During the swipe the next station's channel number and call sign show over the picture, so it reads as tuning, not scrolling.

Radio uses the station's colour

The panel is the station's colour, which is the one place its colour fills a large area, since there's no picture to protect.

The band decides the neighbours

Radio up and down move through the radio band only. You never flip from 88.3 into a TV station.

Locked screen

Both bands keep playing with the phone locked. The lock-screen controls are pause and channel up and down, with the call sign and program as the title.

Every station, three hours at a time.

The guide is a page, not a modal. Stations run down the side in channel order and half hours across the top, with a line for the current time. Selecting a program opens its listing, where you can tune in or set a reminder.

The now line

A single ink line with the time, moving as the evening does. Programs on air sit on the raised colour. Live programs carry the word Live, in red text, never the tally.

Programs that started earlier

A program that began before the window shows a leading marker and its real start time, so "City Council" reads as already under way.

The listing pops over the grid

Selecting a cell outlines it and opens its listing beside it, not a centred modal, so you keep your place in the grid. Reminder is the primary action for anything not yet on.

Switch me over

A broadcast-only idea: at the start time, the player changes channel for you if you're already watching. Off by default.

Carried from

Answers "why is this here?" and lists where else in your market it airs, with times. The primary action is tuning to the station that makes it.

Pledge

Monthly is the default, like a member station. The on-air credit is opt-in and uses the name you choose, and stations read it in their underwriting breaks.

Opencast's share of pledges

The copy assumes pledges go to the station less card fees, with nothing to Opencast. Confirm before this is written anywhere public.

Share a time, not a video

Sharing a program shares when it airs. The link tunes in if it's on, and offers a reminder if it isn't yet.

Remind me

Setting a reminder confirms with a toast and an Undo, not a modal. At the start time it's a notification: "Beat Tape Live is on BEAT 12.1 now."

A thin market.

One question

No account, no genre quiz. Location is used once to pick a market, and the market is all that's stored. Everything else waits until someone wants a preset or a reminder.

Counts are station counts

The market picker shows how many stations each market has. That's the only count the viewer app shows about anything.

Never pad a thin dial

Two stations are shown as two stations, then the nearest market under its own heading and distance. Nothing from elsewhere is passed off as local.

An empty dial recruits

In a thin market the last block invites viewers to start a station, naming the open channels. That's how a market fills.

No presets yet

For a new viewer, the six preset keys show as dashed and empty, with "Tune in to a station and press Add to presets." The layout doesn't change when they fill.
