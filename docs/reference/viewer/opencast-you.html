opencast Reference: sign-in, You and settings
Ref. 04Viewer app, web and phone

An account is optional. Here's what it adds.

Watching never needs an account. Signing in adds four things: presets that follow you to every device and TV, reminders, pledges to stations, and the TVs you watch on. This file covers signing in, the You page that holds those four things, and settings.

Same tokens and components as the home file. Kai M. is signed in, in the Inland Empire, and it's 8:42 pm on Saturday.

Asked for at the moment

Sign-in appears when someone tries to save, remind or pledge, and says which of those it's for. There's no sign-in wall.

Nothing is lost

Presets and reminders made before signing in are kept on the device and offered to the account afterwards.

One place for your stations

Presets, reminders, pledges and TVs live on one page, not scattered through settings.

Settings are for how it behaves

Settings hold behaviour: market, captions, playback, notifications, appearance, privacy. Things you own live on You.

Ordered by use

Presets first because they're used every day, then reminders, then pledges, then TVs. Settings is a link, not a section, because it's about behaviour rather than things you have.

Pledges add up

The section header gives the monthly total. One-time pledges show their date and a receipt, not a manage button.

TVs you can sign out of

A TV running the Opencast app is signed in and can be signed out from here. Cast targets are remembered only for the list; they hold no sign-in.

A station owner sees their station

For someone who runs one, the last block becomes their station: "BEAT 12.1, on air" with Open master control. Everyone else sees how many channels are open.

The eight sections

Account (name, email, sign-in methods, sign out everywhere). Market (yours, and whether nearby markets appear on a thin dial). Watching. Notifications. TVs and casting (lock-screen remote, letting others on the Wi-Fi change channel). Appearance (ground, reduced motion). Privacy. Your data (download, delete).

Examples, not descriptions

Caption size shows a real caption. Data saver says how much data it uses. A setting is easier to choose when you can see what it does.

Privacy says what's kept

Location is used once to pick a market and isn't stored. Watch history is kept for 30 days to resume and power "last channel", and can be cleared. Tuned-in counts that stations see are anonymous.

Opencast never sends notifications about programs you didn't ask about.

A settings section.

Signed out isn't empty

It says the dial works without an account and names the presets already on the phone, so signing in reads as keeping something rather than giving something up.

Settings from the top row

The name row opens settings, a list of the eight sections. Each section is a screen with a back arrow, never a modal.

Notifications have limits in the copy

"For live programs only", "No more than one a month", and a closing line promising nothing unasked. A broadcast app that nags is the thing Opencast is meant to replace.

Quiet hours on by default

A preset going live at midnight doesn't wake anyone. Reminders you set yourself still come through.
