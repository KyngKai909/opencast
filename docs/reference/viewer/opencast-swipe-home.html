Home 02Viewer app, phone, portrait and landscape

The home is the picture. Swipe to change channel.

A redesign of the viewer app's home, for its own focused build pass. The app opens straight onto live TV, full screen. Swiping up and down moves through your presets first, then the rest of the dial in channel order, with resistance as you drag and a snap when you let go. It's the gesture people already know from short-video apps, used for something they know from television: flipping channels.

Only the packaging changes. The player is exactly as specified elsewhere: the tuning static, the corner number, the bug, and the banner with its progress bar, time left and what's next.

Still a dial

The order is fixed: presets, then channel order. Nothing is ranked or recommended, and the same swipe always lands on the same station.

No counts, ever

The right-hand buttons have labels, not numbers. No likes, no views, no viewers.

The drag is the tuning

When the next channel is ready, the drag itself is the transition. Static only appears when it isn't.

You always know where you are

A small line says "Preset 2 of 3" or "Dial, 4 of 5", and crossing from presets into the dial is felt as well as seen.

01Try it

Drag the picture up or down.

A working version in portrait. Drag with a mouse or finger, flick for a fast change, or use the arrow keys. This viewer's presets are BEAT, SAZN and CIVC; after them come the rest of the Inland Empire's TV stations in channel order. PREP 31.1 is slow to load, so you can see the static stand in for it.

Press the blue Tune button and type 7, 24 or 45. Tap the picture to pause, and again to resume behind live; then try "Back to live". In landscape the buttons hide after 3 seconds and come back with any tap.

Presets, then the dial

The first swipes go through the viewer's presets in preset order. Then the rest of the dial in channel order, skipping stations already in the presets. After the last station it wraps back to preset 1.

A detent at the boundary

Dragging from the last preset into the dial meets extra resistance and shows "End of your presets". It takes a slightly longer pull, so nobody leaves their presets by accident.

Live picture, or static

The next and previous stations are preloaded, so dragging usually reveals their live picture. If one isn't ready, it shows the tuning static with its number instead, and keeps the static after the snap until its first frame arrives.

02The moments

At rest, mid-drag, at the boundary, and not ready.

Four stills from the gesture, in portrait. The packaging around the picture is small: the TV or radio band at the top, five labelled buttons on the right, the position line, and a floating bar with the four tabs on the left and the Tune button on the right.

The right-hand buttons

Preset (filled when the station is one), Remind, Pledge, Share and Guide. The same actions the old home had, moved to where thumbs expect them.

TV and Radio at the top

Two bands, like the two feeds short-video apps put at the top. Radio swipes through the radio presets, then the band by frequency, with the needle sweep instead of static.

A floating bar and a Tune button

The four tabs (Watch, Guide, Search, You) float in a pill over the picture, and the main button sits on its own at the right. Tune opens a number pad: type "18" and it tunes to SAZN, with the usual static and corner number. The full dial as a list lives in Guide.

The station page

Tapping the call sign in the banner or the corner opens the station's page. No sideways swipes: one gesture per axis keeps it predictable.

03Pause and back to live

Tap to pause. A button brings you back.

Tapping the picture pauses, and tapping again resumes from the same moment, now behind the broadcast. While you're behind, a "Back to live" button floats above the banner with how far behind you are. Changing channel always joins the new station live.

The tally tells the truth

Paused, the banner's ON AIR sign goes unlit, as the style guide says: the station is still broadcasting, but you're not watching it live.

How far behind

The button counts the gap in minutes and seconds. Behind live is only ever caused by pausing, since there's no scrubbing on a live channel.

Held for 30 minutes

A pause holds for up to 30 minutes, the existing player rule. After that, resuming goes straight back to live.

Buttons don't pause

Taps on the right-hand buttons, the top bar and the floating bar never pause. Only the picture does.

04Tune by number

The Tune button: type a channel, and you're there.

The main button opens a number pad over the picture. Type a channel (18, or 18.2 for a subchannel) or a radio frequency (90.7), and it tunes 2 seconds after you stop typing, or straight away with Tune. It's the same rule as typing a number on the TV remote, and the one thing a swipe can't do: jump anywhere on the dial at once. Try it in the demo above: press the blue button and type 7, 24 or 45.

No band switch needed

TV channels run 2 to 69 and radio 88.1 to 107.9, so the number says which band it is. "18" means TV 18.1; "90.7" means radio.

It tells you before it tunes

As you type, the pad names the station and what's on. A number with no station says so and suggests the nearest, instead of tuning into nothing.

Then the usual tuning

Number entry uses the full channel change: static for at least 300 ms, the corner number, then the banner. Unlike a swipe, the station wasn't preloaded.

From any tab

The Tune button is on every tab. From Guide, Search or You, tuning opens Watch on that station.

05Landscape

The same swipe, sideways on.

Turn the phone and the picture fills the screen. Swiping is still up and down. The tabs and band switch go away; the buttons and position line fade after 3 seconds and come back with a tap.

Up and down, both ways

Landscape keeps the vertical swipe, so the gesture never changes meaning when the phone turns. Horizontal swipes do nothing, which also protects the picture from accidental changes.

Icons only, sideways

In landscape the button labels drop to leave room for the picture. Each keeps its name for screen readers, and a long press shows it.

Casting and mirroring

When the phone is a remote for a TV, this screen becomes the remote as already designed; swiping there changes the TV's channel.

06Guide, Search and You

The other tabs, in the new packaging.

The three other tabs, reworked around the floating bar. They sit on the app's ground instead of the picture, and the channel you were watching keeps playing in a strip above the bar: tap it to go back to Watch, or swipe it down to stop. Guide follows the same order as the swipe, presets first and then the dial.

The guide matches the swipe

Your presets first, then the rest of the dial in channel order, so the guide and the swipe never disagree. A red line marks now; the station you're watching is tinted. Tap a row to tune in; tap a later program to set a reminder.

Search tunes numbers

Typing a number, call sign or frequency puts "Tune to" at the top, the same shortcut as the Tune button. Then stations, and programs on now or later tonight, never ranked by popularity.

Browse by kind, not by trending

Before typing, Search offers kinds of station and recent searches. There's no "trending" or "popular", in keeping with the rule that viewers never see audience numbers.

You is the viewer's own

Presets as six slots in order (the swipe's order), reminders with "switch me over", pledges, TVs, and settings. "Run a station" leads into master control in the same account.

The mini player

Shows the channel's colour, call sign, what's on with time left, and the tally. Its thin line is the program's progress, not a scrub bar.

07Tablet

Every screen on iPad and Android tablets.

The same app on a tablet, in both orientations. Everything works the same way (swipe for channels, tap to pause, the Tune button, the floating bar), but each screen uses the room: portrait keeps the picture 16:9 with the station's colour around it, and landscape puts the live picture beside the guide and search instead of shrinking it into a strip.

Landscape

Never crop the picture

On a phone the picture can fill the screen. On a portrait tablet that would cut off most of a 16:9 broadcast, so it stays 16:9 and the station's colour fills the space, blurred, like light from the screen.

Swipe the whole screen

In portrait the swipe moves the whole screen, picture and glow together, exactly as on a phone.

The pad as a panel

On tablets the Tune pad opens as a panel above the Tune button, in both orientations, so the picture stays visible while typing.

The picture moves over

In landscape, Guide and Search keep the live picture on screen instead of a mini player: beside the guide it's the channel you're watching, beside search it's the top match.

08Gesture and rules

The numbers behind the feel.

For the focused build pass. These sit alongside the player's tuning constants, not in place of them.

DragFollows the finger 1:1 up to 60% of the screen, then a third as far (rubber band)
SnapPast 20% of the screen, or a flick faster than 0.5 px per ms; snaps in 240 ms, easing out
Spring backUnder the threshold, returns in 280 ms with a slight overshoot
Detent (presets to dial, and the wrap)Half the drag distance for the same finger movement, a 35% threshold, a label, and a light haptic tick when crossed
