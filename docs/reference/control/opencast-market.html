"Fits your schedule"

Master control knows BEAT's gaps, library repeats and weakest slots from its audience. Programs whose length and category fit one come first, with an amber tag naming the slot.

Makers are labelled by kind

A station with its channel, a studio, or the Opencast catalog. A station can filter any of them out.

Carried by most is a sort, not a chart

Carriage count is available as a sort, but there's no ranked list or "top programs" page. Popularity is one input, not the front page.

The fit box answers "why this"

Where the program fits BEAT's schedule, in amber, next to the description. When there's an exact fit for a gap, the main button offers it directly.

Rights in the terms

"Made by HALL, confirmed", "Public domain, restored" or "Licensed to Opencast". A station carrying something should know what it's standing on.

Carriers visible to stations

Stations see which other stations carry it, by name within their market. Viewers only ever see the count.

BEAT already carries it

Slow Hours airs on BEAT at 11:00 pm. Carrying it again at 11:40 pm is an extra airing under the same agreement, so no new terms are needed.

03Previewing an episode

A scrub bar, for stations only.

Previewing opens an episode with a normal player: scrubbing, the maker's break points marked in amber, and the listing viewers would see. It's the one place in Opencast with a scrub bar, because a station is checking something, not watching it.

Break points are visible

The amber marks are where the maker placed breaks. A station can see whether they suit its own break rule before carrying.

Previews aren't airings

Nothing about previewing counts as an airing or uses up one of the episode's allowed airings. Makers see how many stations previewed, not who.

04Studios

Make programs without running a station.

A studio is a station with no channel. It never signs on, so master control drops everything about airing: no monitor, log or breaks. What's left is the library, what it offers, who carries it, and earnings. A studio can claim a channel later and become a station.

Want your own channel?Claim a place on the dial and your programs can air on your own station too. Everything here comes with you.

Same account, fewer pages

A studio uses master control with the on-air pages removed. The rail keeps Library, Offers, Carriers, Earnings and Settings, so nothing new has to be learned.

A handle without a channel

Studios have a name and a short handle, but no call sign or channel number, so they never look like something you can tune to.

An upgrade path

Claiming a channel turns a studio into a station, keeping its library, offers, carriers and earnings.

Barter without breaks of your own

A studio's barter share runs on its carriers' stations from the studio's own spot rotation, so studios can still earn from the spot market without a station.

05The Opencast catalog

A shelf any station can fill its nights from.

Opencast's own programming: restored public-domain films, cartoons, newsreels and radio, and US government works like NASA footage, which are public domain by law. It's offered free to every station, underwritten by a sponsor, and later joined by licensed catalogs.

Opencast catalog

Programming Opencast restores, packages and offers to every station at no cost. Rights are checked by Opencast, item by item.

5 series, 1 coming

Free, with a credit

Catalog programs cost stations nothing and leave all break time to the station, except one underwriting credit an hour for the catalog's sponsor. That's where Clear fits naturally.

Rights checked once

Opencast confirms each item's public-domain status or licence, so every station carrying it stands on the same check. The source is listed under each program.

Seeding thin markets

This is what fills a new station's nights before it has its own programming, and what makes a two-station market worth tuning to.

Licensed catalogs

Revenue-share deals with rights holders appear here later, marked "Licensed". How their revenue is split with stations isn't set.

06On the phone

Fill a gap from the market, in two taps.

The dead-air sheet from the flows file offers "Carry from the market". On the phone that opens the market already filtered to programs that fit the gap.

Only what fits

On the phone the market shows programs long enough for the gap, from makers that allow carriage without approval, so carrying works immediately.

One tap carries

Choosing one carries it into the gap on its default deal, with an undo. Changing the deal waits for the laptop.
