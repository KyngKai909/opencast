Translators were drawn in the sign-on flow and live here too, unchanged.

What's permanent says so

Call sign, channel, band and market are locked after first sign-on, and the page explains why instead of hiding the field.

The station sets its ad load

Break length, a cap on spots per hour, and blocked categories are the station's decisions, applied before anything from the spot market.

Three roles

Owner, operator and host. Enough for a collective to share a station, few enough that nobody needs a permissions matrix.

Dead-air alerts can't be off

A station can quiet any notification except the one that says it's about to go silent.

01Identity

Change the look. Keep your place on the dial.

The station's name, description, colour and bug can change any time. Its call sign, channel, band and market are shown locked, each with the reason, because viewers, presets and other stations' carriage agreements depend on them.

How often, how long, and what's never allowed.

The station ID is fixed last

Every break ends with the station ID, so viewers always hear whose station they're on as the program comes back. Everything above it can be reordered.

An hourly cap, shown against TV

A station deciding its own ad load is a real differentiator, so the setting says what broadcast TV does next to what the station chose.

Blocked means invisible

Blocked categories are filtered out of the station's spot market entirely, so an operator can't add one by mistake.

Ads from partners is a backfill

Off by default. When on, it only fills time the station hasn't filled itself, and says plainly that the station doesn't choose these ads and that they pay later. It's a way to earn from open time while the local spot market grows.

Political spots

Political advertising has its own disclosure and equal-time rules in broadcasting. Whether Opencast allows it at all, and on what terms, needs a decision and probably a lawyer.

A host is scoped to blocks

A host can go live only on the blocks they're given, so a guest presenter can run a Tuesday town hall without seeing anything else.

Operators see money, owners move it

Operators can read earnings, because they make the programming decisions that drive them. Only owners change payouts, the team or the station's identity.

Invites expire

After a week, and they can be resent. Nobody is added to a station without accepting with their own sign-in.

One station wallet, many people

The station's Clear account belongs to the station, and people reach it through their roles. Nobody shares a login, and removing someone removes their access at once.
