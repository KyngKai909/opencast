Three parts of master control the flows file pointed at but didn't draw. Live sources: how a live block gets its picture, from an encoder like OBS or straight from a browser or phone. Live happens on schedule

A live source goes out when its block starts, not when someone presses a button. Early connection is a rehearsal only you see.

Stand by, then on air

Before its block, a source shows the amber standby sign and a countdown. The tally lights only when it's actually going out.

Viewers read the listing

Every title and description viewers see comes from listings, and master control flags anything that would air without one.

An item knows its history

Where it's scheduled, where it's carried and every time it aired, so nothing is deleted while it's still in use.

Signal before air

A source can connect any time before its block. Master control shows it's arriving and at what quality, but nothing goes out until the block starts.

The key is hidden by default

A streaming key lets anyone air on BEAT, so it's masked, copied rather than shown, and one button replaces it if it leaks.

No signal, no dead air

If a block starts with nothing arriving, a slate airs and the station is alerted, as the sign-on checks promised. When the signal arrives mid-block, it switches over.

Graphics are on from the start

The bug and lower third are drawn on the preview, so a host sees exactly what goes out. The lower third can be edited or hidden at any time, which is what a town hall with changing speakers needs.

It starts on schedule

The countdown ends and the stream goes out at the block's start time. There's no "Go live" button to forget or press early.

Breaks and ending

Cue a break and End early become available once on air. Ending early fills the rest of the block from the log, never dead air.

Guests

A guest link lets a second person join from their own browser, shown side by side. It's the next thing to design for town halls with remote speakers.

Incomplete listings are flagged

A program with no description still airs, but it's flagged in amber here, in the rail, and in the sign-on checks, because viewers see a blank line in the guide.

A series and an episode

Title belongs to the series and is set once. Each airing can add an episode title and its own description; if it doesn't, the series description is used.

160 characters

The limit is what fits the guide and a TV banner at ten feet. The counter shows it, and the previews prove it.

Carried programs are read-only

Listings for Saturday Reel come from REEL. BEAT can add a local note but can't change the maker's words.

Folders plus "needs attention"

Stations make their own folders. Two automatic ones sit below: items whose rights aren't confirmed, and items still being prepared.

Scheduled and aired, both shown

Future airings come from the log; past ones come from the as-run log, including airings on stations that carry it.

Deleting is guarded

An item in use can't be deleted, and the page says exactly what's using it. Replacing the file keeps its history and schedule.

Stored once, cached before air

Every file is named by its content, so a program carried by 12 stations is stored once. Anything in the next day's log is copied to the playout server ahead of time, so nothing waits on a download at air time.

Your masters, portable

"Export to IPFS" in the item's menu publishes the station's own original there under the same content ID, for a copy that lives outside Opencast. It's the station's choice, per item, because IPFS files are public and can't be taken back.

A speaker list, not a text field

At a town hall the lower third changes every few minutes. Speakers are added once and shown with one tap, so the person holding the phone isn't typing on air.

The phone stays awake

The screen won't sleep while on air, and a low-battery warning appears at 20% with how many minutes are left at the current rate.

A bad connection doesn't end it

If the phone's signal drops, viewers see the stand by slate and the phone reconnects on its own. The station is told if it's gone for more than a minute.
