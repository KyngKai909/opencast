Ref. 10Master control, web and phone

When someone says "that's mine."

Two things that protect creators. Rights claims: someone says a station aired their work without permission, the item comes off air everywhere it airs, and the station either removes it or answers the claim. Claimable stations: Opencast runs a station of a creator's work with their permission, and hands it over when they're ready.

Same master control shell and tokens. BEAT 12.1 has one open claim, on an item imported from a link.

Off air first, questions after

A claim takes the item off air straight away, on every station airing it. The log fills the gap, so nobody hears silence.

Two honest ways out

Remove it, or answer the claim with the same three reasons the station gave when it uploaded. No other paths, no arguing in messages.

A record that counts

Upheld claims stay on a station's record for a year, and repeated ones have consequences. The station always sees its own standing.

Permission before a station

Opencast only runs a station of someone's work after they've said yes or their licence allows it, and hands it over with everything it earned, held in escrow until then.

Standing in plain numbers

Open claims, upheld claims this year, and what the threshold is. A station should never be surprised by a consequence.

Removing isn't "upheld"

A station that removes an item when asked isn't marked against. Only claims that go against a station after it answered count toward the record.

The repeat policy

A platform needs a policy for repeat infringers to keep its legal protection. "3 upheld in a year pauses carriage offers" is a placeholder; the real thresholds and what follows need a lawyer.

03Answering a claim

The same three reasons as at upload.

Answering asks which of the three rights statements is true, the same ones the station chose when it uploaded, plus what backs it up. The answer goes to the claimant, and the station's identity goes with it.

Same words as upload

The three statements match the library's rights question, so a station is asked to stand by what it already said.

Identity travels with it

A counter-notice has to identify the person answering. The checkbox says so in one sentence before anything is sent.

Legal wording

The timeline and this form follow the shape of a US copyright takedown and counter-notice. The exact wording and deadlines need a lawyer before launch; I'm not one.

04A claim on a carried program

Pulled from the maker and every carrier at once.

When a claim hits a program other stations carry, it comes off air on all of them. The maker sees each carrier and what replaced the episode; carriers get a notice naming the episode and the gap that was filled, without the claimant's details.

Carriers were told again when it returned. Their logs picked it up from the next scheduled airing.

Pull everywhere, fill everywhere

One claim reaches every station airing the item, and each station's log fills the gap with the next episode or its usual fill, so carriers never go silent over someone else's claim.

Carriers don't need to act

The notice says what was replaced and that nothing is required. The dispute stays between the maker and the claimant.

It comes back the same way

When a claim resolves in the maker's favour, the episode returns to every carrier's schedule from its next airing, and they're told.

05Claiming a station

"We've been running your station. It's yours when you want it."

A claimable station is one Opencast's team runs from a creator's work, after the creator agreed or because their work is already published under a licence that allows it. When the creator is ready, this page hands it over: what it has aired, its audience, the money held for them in escrow, and three steps to take ownership.

It starts from the yes

The page opens with the date the creator agreed. A station of someone's work never exists before that.

Proof, not paperwork

Identity is shown by connecting the account the work was published from, not by uploading documents.

Everything comes with it

Call sign, channel, presets, carriage, and everything it earned, paid out of escrow to the wallet made when they sign in. The team stays on as operators until the new owner removes them.

A balance anyone can check

The escrow contract and the station's ID are on the claim page and the station page, so a creator can check what's held, and that the contract can only pay them, before trusting the claim.

Why 72 hours

An approved claim waits three days in public before paying out, so if someone other than the creator got verified, it can be stopped.

Saying no is one link

If the creator doesn't want it, it's signed off within a day and paid out. No questions, no retention flow.

Monitor

101.9

Always labelled

While Opencast runs a station, master control says so at the top, and the station's public page says "Run by Opencast for Marcus Reyes". Viewers are never misled about who's behind it.

Held earnings

Everything a claimable station earns, after the same fees and pool share as any station, is paid into an escrow contract under its station ID. It can go to the creator when they claim or ask to stop, or after 3 years unclaimed to the creator fund. The contract can't pay Opencast.
