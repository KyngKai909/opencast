Ref. 02Broadcaster app, web first

Master control: three flows.

The station side of Opencast. Signing on for the first time, carrying another station's program, and filling a break from the spot market. Master control is built for a laptop, since running a station is desk work; the phone covers watching over it once it's on air.

Same tokens and components as the style guide and the viewer app. It's 8:42 pm on a Saturday, and BEAT 12.1 is the station throughout.

Dead air is the emergency

Every screen knows how long until the log runs out. Gaps are shown before sign-on and warned about before they reach air.

Rights before air

Nothing airs until its station confirms it can air it. Material imported from a link can air, but can't be offered for carriage.

Terms before commitment

Carriage and spots show what each side gets, in a few lines, before anything is added to the log.

The log is the truth

Everything a station does ends up as lines in the program log, to the second, with the same codes everywhere.

ASign on for the first time

From nothing to on air in five steps.

The repo's core path, set in order: claim a place on the dial, fill the library, build the log, connect translators, sign on. Each step saves as you go, and the rail shows what's done. Signing in with a wallet happens before step one and isn't shown here.

The channel is a real choice

Numbers are scarce per market on purpose, like the dial they copy. Showing the taken ones makes the dial feel real and nudges people to claim early.

What's permanent says so

Call sign and channel don't change after sign-on, because viewers and other stations learn them. The line under the title says that before anyone types.

Contrast is checked, not suggested

A colour that fails 4.5:1 against white can't be saved, with the reason shown. Station colours carry white text everywhere.

Preview in their words

The right pane shows the station exactly as the viewer app will: on the dial, as a bug and as the station card.

"Preparing for air"

Not "processing" or "compressing". The repo's FFmpeg step becomes a progress bar with a plain status, and a finished item says Ready for air.

Type is set per item

Program, spot, underwriting, bumper or station ID, shown with the log's own codes. Anything under a minute is guessed as a bumper and can be changed.

Rights are part of the item

An item with unconfirmed rights can sit in the library but can't be placed in the log. The row says so in standby amber.

Because it came from a link, it can air on BEAT but can't be offered to other stations for carriage. If the owner asks, it comes off air the same day.

Three honest answers

No "I agree" checkbox. Asking which of three things is true makes someone think for a second, and the answer is kept with the item.

Link imports stay local

Anything from a link airs only on the station that imported it. That keeps the syndication market clean without banning the import tool.

Takedowns

How a rights complaint reaches a station and pulls an item from the log, including from stations carrying it, needs its own flow.

Planned off air hours aren't dead air: no warnings, nothing fills them, and viewers see when you're back.

Programs are placed in whole minutes; breaks are placed for you.

A timeline, not a playlist

The repo's queue becomes the day it will actually be. Each program has a start time, breaks are drawn where they'll fall, and a gap is drawn as dead air.

Breaks come from a rule

The repo's three break modes, in plain words. The station sets the rule once; the log places each break and puts a station ID in it.

Three ways to fill a gap

Repeat your own programs, carry someone else's, or sign off. All three are fine; dead air is the only wrong answer.

Off air is a choice, dead air is a mistake

A station can sign off on a schedule, like every night from 2:00 to 6:00 am. Those hours are off air: the guide shows them, viewers see when the station is back, and nothing warns or fills. Dead air is only an unplanned gap.

Build a day once

"Repeat this day" makes the day a template: every Saturday, weekdays or every day. Changing one date changes only that date; changing the template changes every future repeat.

Live shows are blocks too

Beat Tape Live at 9:00 is a block with an ingest source instead of a file. Master control switches to it at 9:00 and back when it ends.

Skippable on purpose

This is the wedge from earlier: a station can use master control only to run its YouTube channel. But nobody should be blocked from signing on by it.

Program and preview

The two monitors a real control room has: what's on air now, and what's next. The tally sits on the program monitor and in the header.

The audience count lives here

Viewers never see counts. Stations do, because spots are sold on them. It's labelled "Tuned in", not views.

The market on the Monitor

Two programs from the syndication market that fit this station's gaps or repeats, with the slot each fits. The market comes to the station instead of waiting to be browsed.

Cue a break now

For live programs, the host decides when the break goes. It fills from the break's rotation and returns to the live source afterwards.

Three deals, one sentence each

Barter, cash and cash plus barter, each described by who fills which break time and what it costs. The price is always on the right, including $0.00.

Six facts, no contract

The whole agreement is the choice plus three lines: airings, attribution and how it ends. The same principle as the Clear merchant terms.

Who approves a carrier

Whether REEL approves each station that carries it, or opens the program to any station automatically. Automatic is simpler; approval protects a producer's brand.

CFill a break

Tonight BEAT has open time in four breaks. The station browses spots local businesses have listed, adds three to its rotation, and the log fills the breaks from it. Break time that belongs to REEL under barter is shown but can't be sold.

Stations choose, advertisers don't place

The market is a list a station picks from. Nothing airs on BEAT that BEAT didn't add.

Two ways to pay

Per 1,000 tuned in, or a flat rate an airing. Both are shown as the business listed them, never as an estimate of what BEAT will make.

Conversion the TV way

A code or QR on screen, instead of a click. The business sees redemptions; the station sees airings.

Opencast's share

The cut of spot revenue and how the pool splits it aren't set yet. The pane says a share is taken and where to see it, without a number.

A dead-air warning, on a night the log runs short.

Warnings come early

At 30 minutes and again at 12. Each opens straight into the same three choices the log offers, so it's solved from the lock screen in two taps.

If nobody answers

At the gap, master control repeats from the library automatically rather than going dark. The station is told afterwards what aired.

Two buttons, both ghost

Cue a break and Sign off are the only actions on the phone, and neither is primary, because neither should be pressed by accident.
