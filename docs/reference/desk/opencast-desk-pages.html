01Rights claims

Every claim on every station, and what's due next.

Opencast doesn't decide who's right. It follows the process, keeps the record, and restores or removes on the dates.

Carriers count

A claim on a carried program pulls it from every station airing it. The carriers column shows how far a claim reached, and they're all restored together.

Not every claim is copyright

A privacy complaint, like someone filmed without consent, follows its own path. The row says so rather than forcing it into the copyright timeline.

Repeat limit

Stations with upheld claims are counted against the repeat-infringer policy. The number that triggers action is set in Settings.

Deadlines and wording

The answer window, the counter-notice waiting period and every word the claimant and station receive need a lawyer's review before launch. The dates here are placeholders set in Settings.

02Reserved call signs

Names and numbers held for the waitlist.

People who join the waitlist as a station can reserve a call sign, and optionally a channel, in their market. This page shows every reservation with its state and end date, and flags the ones that need a decision: two people wanting the same name, or a name Opencast won't allow.

Showing 5 of 26. Reservations last 120 days, or until the invite is accepted or declined.

First come, with a person deciding ties

When two people reserve the same name, the earlier one usually wins, but the desk decides and offers the other a suggestion. The fairest answer isn't always the earliest.

Names Opencast won't allow

Four letters starting with K or W look like FCC stations, so they're refused with a suggestion. So are names that impersonate a real station, brand or person.

Held, then released

A reservation ends after 120 days unless the person signs on or the desk extends it, so the dial isn't blocked by names nobody uses. When call sign licenses exist, this is where their renewals will show.

Invite in batches

"Invite the next 10" sends invites in reservation order, so a market can open at the pace the desk can support.

03Catalog sponsors

One credit an hour, sold by market.

Catalog programs keep one sponsor credit an hour. By default it thanks Clear everywhere. The desk can sell it market by market and series by series, so a sponsor in the Inland Empire is thanked only on Inland Empire stations. Each row is one sponsor, where it's credited, what it pays, and how many credits aired.

Clear fills the gaps

Clear is thanked anywhere no other sponsor has bought the slot, so the catalog is never unsponsored and Clear gets steady, honest visibility.

Sold where it airs

A slot is a series in a market. The row says how often the series airs there, which is what a sponsor is buying.

Where catalog money goes

Whether catalog sponsorship funds Opencast, the co-op pool, or the creator fund, and in what split, isn't decided. The share is shown as not set.

Open decisions live here

Everything marked Open in the other files has its switch on this page, showing "Not set yet" until it's decided. Nothing is hidden in code.

Changes have dates

A new price or share starts on a date, never retroactively, and statements show which value applied to each line.

Team roles

Admins change rules and signers. Rights reviewers do the second check on catalog items and handle claims. Market leads run the pipeline and reservations in their markets.

Markets and signers

Markets holds each market's numbering ranges (TV 2 to 69 with subchannels, radio 88.1 to 107.9), so they can grow without code. Escrow signers shows the multi-sig verifier keys, and changing one needs the others to approve.
