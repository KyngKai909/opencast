Ref. 13TV mode, update

The same TV on an Apple TV, and the settings behind it.

Two additions to the TV mode file. First, the route for Apple TVs and AirPlay TVs: the iPhone app mirrors, but instead of copying the phone's screen it draws TV mode on the TV and keeps the remote on the phone. Second, TV settings: what someone on a sofa might want to change, set in ten-foot type, including a sleep timer.

Same tokens and ten-foot rules as the TV mode file. Always a dark ground on the TV.

The TV looks the same

Mirrored, cast or in the app, the TV shows the same banner, guide and radio screens. Only the hint row says where the controls are.

Say the extra step plainly

The app can't start mirroring by itself, so it shows exactly where the button is, once, and notices when it's on.

Warn before it breaks

Mirroring stops if the phone locks. The phone says so before it happens and helps restart it after.

Settings a sofa needs

Captions, how the remote behaves, a sleep timer, and who can change the channel. Nothing a TV viewer would never touch.

01Mirroring to an Apple TV

TV mode on the TV, not a copy of the phone.

When an iPhone mirrors to an Apple TV or an AirPlay TV, Opencast draws its own TV screen there: the full picture, the same banner on every channel change, the same guide. The phone keeps the remote. The hint row names the phone and says to keep Opencast open.

The same screen as casting

It's the TV mode file's watching screen. Only the chip changes: "Mirrored from Kai's iPhone" instead of "Playing from".

Why the hint is different

A cast TV keeps playing when the phone locks; a mirrored one doesn't. So the hint asks to keep Opencast open rather than pointing at the controls.

Picture quality

Mirroring re-encodes the screen, so the picture is a little softer than casting and runs a moment behind. For broadcast programming it's fine; an Apple TV app would remove the difference later.

02The iPhone side

Show where the button is, then get out of the way.

Choosing an Apple TV in "Watch on" opens a one-time guide to turning on Screen Mirroring. When the app notices mirroring has started, it switches to the remote by itself. If the phone locks and mirroring stops, reopening the app offers to start it again.

After the phone locked.

A picture of the button

A small drawing of Control Center with Screen Mirroring highlighted, then three steps. Shown the first time only, unless the person asks for it again.

It notices by itself

When the TV connects, the phone switches to the remote without a tap, and the TV switches to Opencast's TV screen.

The warning lives with the remote

"Keep this screen open", with battery and time left, on the remote itself. The app also keeps the screen from dimming while mirroring.

Choose a TV that fits

In "Watch on", Chromecasts say "Cast", Apple and AirPlay TVs say "Mirror", and TVs with the Opencast app say "Open the app". Each goes to the route that works best there.

03One TV mode, three inputs

Built once, driven three ways.

TV mode is one web app. What changes between an Opencast TV app, a Chromecast and an iPhone mirroring is where its instructions come from. Building it with one input adapter means all three stay identical.

The five sections

Watching. Remote and phones (who on the Wi-Fi can change the channel, pairing a phone). Picture and sound (quality, audio level evening-out). Account (signed in as, sign out of this TV). About this TV.

Left and right change a value

The focused row shows arrows; ◀ and ▶ step through its options. No dropdowns or keyboards anywhere in TV settings.

The radio band is separate by default

Channel up stays on the TV band unless this is on, so a viewer flipping channels doesn't land on a still frequency screen by surprise.

A real caption preview

Caption size is set against a real line of captions, at ten feet, like the phone settings.

05Sleep timer

Fall asleep to it, like TV.

A sleep timer is the setting people use most on a TV that's always on. It's in the menu rail, and on the phone remote. "End of this program" is the default choice, because that's what people usually mean.

Times, not just durations

Each option says the clock time it ends, so nobody has to do the maths half asleep.

A gentle end

The sound fades over the last minute with a notice offering 30 more minutes. Any button press cancels it.

It stops Opencast, not the TV

The app can only stop itself. On a TV app it returns to the TV's home screen; while casting or mirroring, the stream ends.
