opencast Reference: TV mode
Ref. 03Opencast on the television

Turn it on and it's on.

Opencast running on the TV itself, working like a cable box. It reaches the TV two ways, and both use these screens. As a TV app (web code on Android TV, Google TV, Fire TV and TV browsers), it's driven by the TV's remote. When casting (Chromecast or AirPlay from the phone), the TV shows the picture and the banner, and the phone becomes the remote.

Frames are 1920 by 1080 with the broadcast safe area from the style guide. The TV is always on the dark ground, whatever the switch at the top says. It's 8:42 pm and BEAT 12.1 is on.

It opens tuned in

The last channel, full screen, within a second or two. No home screen, no profile picker.

Up and down is channels

On the picture, the arrows change channel even on remotes without CH buttons. Everything else is one press away.

Nothing to type

Signing in and pledging hand off to the phone with a code or a QR. The TV never shows a keyboard.

Ten feet away

Nothing under 21px at 1080p, one focus ring in signal cyan, and everything inside the 90% title-safe area.

02Watching and changing channel

A picture, and a banner when you need it.

Most of the time the screen is only the station's picture and its bug. Changing channel or pressing OK brings up the banner for five seconds: who you're watching, what's on, how far in, and what's next.

The banner is the whole interface

Ident, what's on with how far in, what's next, the clock and the tally. It hides after five seconds. It covers the station's bug while it's up, since it carries the same ident.

A progress bar, not a scrub bar

It shows where the program is, not something you can move. The start and end times sit at each end.

Hints teach the remote

The bottom row names the three most useful keys and where Back will take you. After a week of use, the row hides itself.

Channel changes are instant

The next and previous stations are warmed up in the background, so ▲ ▼ switches picture in well under a second, like cable did.

Tune by number

Typing 1 then 2 shows 12 with the .1 filled in and the station it will tune to. It tunes after a second and a half, or at once on OK.

Wrong numbers

A channel with no station shows "No station on 13" and the nearest two, then stays on the current channel.

The old picture stays

CIVC keeps playing until BEAT is ready, so typing never produces a black screen.

03The guide

The cable guide, with the channel still playing.

The guide covers the screen but keeps the current channel playing in a window, the way cable guides did. The focused program's details sit at the top left. Arrows move through the grid; OK on a program that's on now tunes to it, and OK on a later one opens its options.

Options for later programs

The same choices as the web listing, stacked as big buttons with the primary focused. Back closes it and returns to the same cell.

No record button

Opencast is a broadcast, so there's nothing to record. If on-demand is ever added, it appears here, not as a DVR.

Reminders reach the TV

At 8:59 a small card slides in over whatever's on: "Beat Tape Live is starting on BEAT 12.1. OK to switch."

06Casting and the phone remote

The TV shows it. The phone runs it.

When the phone app casts to a Chromecast or AirPlay TV, the TV plays the station full screen and shows the same banner on every change. The phone turns into a remote: channel rocker, number keys, presets, guide and info.

Keypad.

The cast button

It sits in the player's top bar and opens a short list of TVs on the Wi-Fi. It also points out that the installed TV app is better than casting where it's available.

A rocker, like a remote

Two rockers: channel on the left, play and last on the right, with the current channel between them and its neighbours named underneath.

Everything a remote has

Guide, Info, a keypad and Last, plus the six presets, so a phone does everything the cable-style remote in section 01 does.

On the lock screen

While casting, the lock screen shows channel up, channel down and pause, so changing channel doesn't need the app open.

Casting to Roku and Apple TV

AirPlay reaches Apple TV. Roku has no casting target for web apps; Roku viewers are reached through FAST packaging of stations instead.
