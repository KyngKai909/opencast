Biz 05Opencast for business and master control, web and phone

No spot yet? Have a station make one.

Most local businesses don't have a commercial. A production order asks a station, or Opencast's own studio, to make one: the business writes a brief, the maker quotes, the business pays into a hold, the maker delivers, and the money is released when the business approves. The maker is paid for the work whether or not the spot ever airs on their station.

Same tokens and shells. Orange Street Coffee orders a holiday gift card spot from BEAT 12.1.

Paid before work starts

The price is held from the business's balance when it accepts the quote. The maker knows the money is there before making anything.

Released on approval

Approving releases it to the maker. Not answering for 7 days after delivery releases it too, so a maker is never left waiting.

Changes are counted

The quote says how many rounds of changes are included. Notes are pinned to a moment in the spot, not written as an email.

Straight into a spot

An approved order becomes a spot in the business's Spots, already checked, ready for a rate and budget.

Held, not charged

The money moves from available to held, like airings, and shows in the balance's movements. It isn't spent until approval.

Both ends protected

The maker is paid on approval or after 7 quiet days. The business gets everything back if the delivery date passes and it cancels.

If they can't agree

After the included changes, either side can ask Opencast to review. A disputed order is held until then, never refunded or released automatically.

Notes on the timeline

Each note is pinned to a moment, shown as an amber mark on the scrub bar. The maker sees the same marks.

A wrong detail is a fix, not a change

Mistakes against the brief, like the brunch hours, don't use up the round of changes. The maker can mark a note as "our mistake".

Auto-approval on October 2 is written on the page, not buried in terms.
