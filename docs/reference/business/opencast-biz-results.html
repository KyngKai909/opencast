The same log as stations

Airings come from the as-run log, the one stations are paid from. The business and the station always see the same airing.

Tuned in, added up, said as that

The number is people tuned in at each airing, summed. It's never called "reach" or "unique viewers", because it isn't.

Customers counted where they pay

In person, online, or marked by hand. Scans alone aren't customers.

Cost per customer

The number a local business actually wants: what each redeemed code cost, by station.

Three steps, honestly labelled

Scans, saves, uses. Only uses are called customers, and a line says plainly that not every customer who came in because of a spot will use the code.

Counted where the sale happens

Clear Pay counts in-person uses by itself; a connected online checkout counts promo codes used there. A business with neither marks uses in the app. Clear Pay is where Opencast and Clear meet for a local storefront.

The saved offer names the station

"Saved from BEAT 12.1" on the customer's phone. The station gets credit where the customer can see it.

Stations see their own count

In master control, a spot's row shows how many customers came from airings on that station. It helps stations pick spots that work for their viewers, without seeing anything about the business beyond that.

Starts and ends with the balance

Started, added, spent, ended, with available and held shown under the ending figure, so the statement reconciles to the balance page.

Short-airing returns are shown, not hidden

They're already netted into what was spent, and listed separately so a business sees the rule working.

Tax treatment

Whether a sponsorship or spot is a deductible business expense is for the business's accountant. The statement is laid out so they don't have to ask for anything else.
