Biz 02Opencast for business, web and phone

Make a spot once. Stations decide where it airs.

A business uploads a spot, Opencast checks it will air cleanly and adds its on-screen code, the business sets a rate, a budget and who it's for, and it's listed in the spot market. From there, stations choose it. This file follows a spot from upload through its states: in review, listed, in rotation, paused and ended.

Same tokens and business shell as file 1. Orange Street Coffee's fall menu spot.

Checked, then fixed for them

Length, picture and safe areas are checked on upload. What Opencast can fix, like loudness, it fixes; what needs the business, it shows on the frame.

The code is added

Every spot gets its own on-screen code and QR, placed inside the safe area, so results can be counted without the business designing for it.

Targeting says who can pick it

Targeting doesn't place a spot; it decides which stations see it in their market. The match list names them.

A pause explains itself

Why it paused, what still aired, what each station did about the gap, and the one action that resumes it.

Safe areas on the frame

The style guide's action safe and title safe, drawn over the actual spot. The problem is shown where it is, not described.

Fix it for them where possible

Loudness is levelled automatically, because a spot louder than the program is the most common complaint about TV ads. Captions are generated. Only what needs a creative decision is left to the business.

A way out without re-editing

"Shrink to fit" scales the spot to bring everything inside title safe, for a business that has no editor to go back to.

The code is Opencast's

Each spot gets its own code and QR, generated and placed by Opencast. Redemptions come back to the Results page without the business tracking anything.

A rate turned into dollars

"$1.40 to $3.30 an airing" on the matched stations, from their real tuned-in numbers, so $8.00 per 1,000 means something.

Budget turned into days

The total and daily cap become "about 5 airings a day, for about 25 days", with the available balance beside it.

Online businesses target markets

A business with no location chooses whole markets instead of a distance. The rest of targeting, kinds of station and times of day, works the same.

Stations are named

Targeting ends in a list of real stations, including the ones left out and why. A business knows exactly whose market it's in.

Stations still choose

Being matched puts the spot in a station's market. Whether it airs is the station's decision; the copy never says "your spot will air on".

Held airings are part of the story

"6 airings already held still aired" tells the business its last money bought real airings after the pause, not nothing.

What each station did

Backup rotation, another spot, or station ID. It's the honest picture of what pausing costs: the slots don't wait.

Budget is not balance

Raising a budget uses money already available; it doesn't charge anything. The line under the button says so.

Resuming tells stations

When it's back, each station that had it gets "Fall menu is back, add it again?" They choose whether to put it back in rotation.

Already handled, shown as done

With a backup rotation, the notice reports what was filled rather than asking for action. "Change" is there if the operator prefers something else.

Coming back isn't automatic

A resumed spot doesn't reappear in a station's breaks by itself; the station chooses. The two sides stay in step: "Waiting for you" on the business side, "Add it back" on the station's.
