Biz 01Opencast for business, web and phone

A balance that pays stations before they air.

The first file of the business side: a local business signs up, funds its balance, and from then on sees three numbers that explain everything: what's available, what's held for airings stations have already scheduled, and what's been spent. The rule behind it is the one from the platform prompt: money is held when a station puts a spot in a break, and paid when it airs.

Same tokens as the rest of Opencast, in the house style. Orange Street Coffee in Redlands, whose spots already appear in BEAT's breaks. Saturday, September 26.

Three numbers

Available, held and spent. Every other money screen in the app is a breakdown of one of them.

The rate is the price

What a business sets is what it pays. Nothing is added on top; card fees are Stripe's, at cost, and bank transfers through Clear are free.

Unheld money is theirs

Anything not held for a scheduled airing can be taken out at any time.

Warned in days, not dollars

Low balance is said as how many days of airings are left, because that's the decision a business actually makes.

Category before anything else

Stations block categories in their settings. Saying how many stations can carry a business's category up front avoids a surprise after the first spot is made.

A location, a service area, or online

A storefront gives an address, a service business a city and radius, an online business its markets. Stations see the city, or "Online". A street address is never shown.

Clear is optional

A Clear business account makes funding instant and free, and it's offered once here. A business without one funds by bank or card.

What happens to your money

  1. It sits in your balance. Nothing is spent by adding it.
  2. A station schedules your spot, and the price of that airing is held.
  3. It airs, and the held amount is paid to the station. If an airing is cut short, the hold comes back.
  4. Anything not held is yours to take out at any time.

The rate you set is what you pay. Opencast adds nothing on top.

Fees shown in dollars

The card option shows its fee on this amount, $7.55, rather than a percentage. Bank transfers and Clear say "No fee".

Amounts in airings

One line turns $250 into roughly how many airings it buys, which is the unit a business thinks in.

The four-line promise

Held when scheduled, paid when aired, returned if cut short, withdrawable if unheld. It's the same rule stations are told, from the other side.

Opencast's share

Taken from what the station receives, not added to what the business pays, so the rate a business sets is the price it pays. Stations see the share in their earnings.

Days, then dollars

"About 44 days of airings" comes before any dollar figure the business would have to work out. The warning thresholds are also set in days.

Holds are visible

Held money is amber, the same as standby everywhere else in Opencast: committed, not yet on air. It's shown as its own line in the movements, then paid or returned.

Short airings return money by themselves

When the as-run log shows a spot aired only :12 of :30, the unaired part comes back without the business having to ask.

Per-thousand airings show their count

Every airing line gives the tuned-in number and the rate, so a business can check the maths the same way stations can on their statements.
