Biz 06Opencast for business, web and phone

Who the business is, and who can do what.

The last business file: the profile stations see, a team with three roles who sign in from anywhere, money and receipts for the bookkeeper, notifications, and connections. It mirrors station settings in master control, with the same layout and the same kind of roles. It works the same for a storefront, a service business that travels, or a business that only sells online.

Same tokens and business shell. Orange Street Coffee.

Say what stations see

The profile marks which details stations and viewers see, and which stay private.

Three roles

Owner, manager and viewer. Everyone signs in from anywhere; viewers see results without touching money.

Money warnings stay on

Like dead air for stations, a business can't turn off the warning that its spots are about to pause.

Connections are optional

Clear makes funding free. Clear Pay or an online checkout counts codes where customers pay. Everything works without them.

Changing your category re-checks which stations can carry you. Spots already in rotation stay until a station decides otherwise.

Who sees each field

A short label next to each field: everyone, stations, viewers, or private. No privacy page to find.

Not every business has a door

A location, a service area (a plumber covering 20 miles from Riverside), or online (a shop that ships anywhere). Online businesses target whole markets instead of a distance, and stations see "Online" instead of a city.

A second location

A business opening in Colton adds it here, and spots can target either location's distance. It's the reason the Colton spot exists.

Category changes are checked

A new category may be blocked by some stations. It's re-checked, and existing rotations are left to the stations.

Viewers for the people around a business

A bookkeeper, a partner or an investor can see what the money bought without being able to spend it.

Managers can spend, not withdraw

A manager, including someone at an agency, can run spots, add money and approve orders on the business's behalf, but only the owner can take money out or change where it comes from.

The same shape as stations

Three roles, a permissions table, and "Last in", like station settings. People who use both apps meet one pattern.

Adding money isn't an expense; spending it on airings is. Receipts for money added say so, so a bookkeeper doesn't count it twice.

Two kinds of receipt

Money added is a prepayment; airings, sponsorships and orders are the expenses. The statement is the document that matters for books, and the list says which is which.

The EIN is masked

Stored for receipts, shown only as its last four, editable by the owner.

Receipts to a bookkeeper

An address for receipts separate from the owner's, so a bookkeeper gets them without an account.

The locked warning

"Spots about to pause" can't be turned off, for the same reason stations can't turn off dead-air warnings: it's the one alert that protects what's on air.

Per person

Each team member sets their own. Viewers start with only the weekly summary.

Count codes where customers pay

Clear Pay in person, an online checkout for online sales, or both. All optional; without them, codes can be marked as used in the app.
