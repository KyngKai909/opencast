How carriage stays cleanFor everyone
  1. 01
    Every program has a rights record

    Who made it, who owns the music and footage, and the licence it airs under. No record, no carriage.

  2. 02
    The maker decides where it airs

    Markets, TV or radio band, time of day, how many airings a week, and which outlets beyond the dial.

  3. 03
    Every carrier is approved

    Approve each request, or let any station that fits carry it. A decline tells the station the reason you pick, never a note.

  4. 04
    Ending is fair to both sides

    Give notice and the carrier keeps the show until the end date. Nobody wakes up to dead air.

  5. 05
    A claim stops it everywhere at once

    If someone claims a program, it comes off every station's schedule while it's sorted out. The channel keeps running.

Questions makers ask6 answers
Do I need a station to offer a show?

No. Studios offer programs without a channel. If you run a station too, your shows can still travel to others.

What can I offer?

A series, a one-off, or a weekly live show. Live programs air at the same time on every carrier; recorded ones air when each station schedules them.

How should I price it?

A revenue share costs a new station nothing up front, so it's the easiest yes. A cash rate suits shows a station already wants. You can change terms at any time; new terms apply to new carriers.

Who pays if a carrier's balance runs out?

Cash is charged when an episode airs, from the carrier's earnings first. A carrier that can't pay stops airing new episodes, and you're told.

Can I take a show back?

Yes. Stop offering it and no one new can carry it. Stations already carrying it keep it until the notice you gave ends.

What about the public-domain catalog?

The network keeps a catalog of public-domain films and recordings that any station can carry for free.
