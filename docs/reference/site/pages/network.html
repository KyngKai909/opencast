The networkAbout

A network,not a feed.

Local TV used to work like this: a few stations in every town, each with a number on the dial, paid by the businesses down the street, carrying the best shows from everywhere else. We're rebuilding that for anyone with something worth airing.

Why a network

Broadcast TV had a schedule, a dial, local ads and shows that travelled. Then the internet gave everyone a channel and took all of that away. We kept what worked and dropped the licence.

02The dialNumbers and call signs

Every station
gets a number.

A number you can remember and a call sign you can say out loud. It's how people find you again tomorrow.

12.4

    How numbers workFive rules
    1. 01
      A number in your market

      TV from 2.1 to 69.9, radio from 88.2 to 107.8. Each number belongs to one station in each market.

    2. 02
      A call sign that's yours

      Three to five letters, unique across the whole network. Pick it once: it can't change after you sign on.

    3. 03
      Nothing that looks official

      No four-letter signs starting with K or W, which look like real FCC stations, and no network or brand names.

    4. 04
      Hold one before you're ready

      Reserve a call sign and a number on the waitlist. It's held for 120 days, with a reminder before it lapses.

    5. 05
      Sign off for good, and it's kept

      Your call sign stays yours for a year. The number goes back to the market after 90 days.

    06StandardsSeven of them

    Rules every
    station keeps.

    Few of them, written plainly, the same for the biggest station and the smallest.

    A rule changeDated
    Takes effectFrom a date, never before today
    BackdatedNever
    The old valueKept, with who changed it
    Past statementsRead exactly as they did
    On every stationFor everyone
    1. 01
      Every break has a station ID

      Viewers always know what they're watching. A station can choose how often, never "never".

    2. 02
      Every program has a rights record

      Who made it, who owns the music and footage, and the licence it airs under. No record, no air.

    3. 03
      A claim stops a program everywhere

      Anyone can file one. The program comes off every station at once while the station answers. The channel keeps running.

    4. 04
      Repeat claims pause carriage

      Three upheld claims in a year and a station can't offer shows to others until it's sorted.

    5. 05
      Ratings and advisories on every show

      From TV-Y to TV-MA, with language and mature flags. Children's programs never get personalized ads.

    6. 06
      Breaks stay short

      Each station sets its own breaks, starting from three minutes of spots an hour, and can turn whole categories of ads away.

    7. 07
      A bill never takes a channel dark

      Run short and you stay on the air. Relays and live hours pause after a 14-day grace; the dial never does.

    07WhereMarkets we're filling first
    Markets are sets of ZIP codes. Your market decides which stations come first on your dial.
    Questions people ask7 answers
    Who runs the network?

    A small team in Southern California. We run the network desk: rights checks, the catalog, markets and the rules on this page. Stations run themselves.

    Where are you starting?

    The Inland Empire first, then Los Angeles and Orange County. Anyone anywhere can sign on; those are the markets we're filling first.

    Is it really free to watch?

    Yes. No account, no subscription. Spots, sponsors and pledges pay for the stations, and stations pay for the storage they use.

    Can I watch on my TV?

    On Android TV, Google TV and Fire TV in the browser, or cast from your phone and use the phone as the remote.

    Do I own my station?

    Your number, call sign, schedule and earnings are yours. You decide what airs and who can carry it.

    Can my city or school be on the dial?

    Yes. Public meetings and public access can be listed from their own stream, with no ads, or run as a full station.

    Is the software open?

    That's the plan: the apps and the code, open for anyone to read and improve. When the money contracts go live, every payment they make can be read by anyone.
